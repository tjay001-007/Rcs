--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 0.  WHAT THE EQM BLOCK IS, AND WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The "equations of motion" block is the aircraft's PLANT: given the external
forces and moments acting on the airframe, it integrates Newton-Euler rigid-body
dynamics forward in time and reports where the aircraft is, how fast it is going,
and how it is oriented. Everything else in the model (FCS, aero, propulsion) only
produces forces and moments; the EqM block turns those into MOTION.

Most Simulink aircraft models use a FLAT-EARTH 6-DOF block (Aerospace Blockset
"6DOF (Euler Angles)" or "6DOF ECEF"). This model uses a CUSTOM rotating-Earth
ECEF formulation written as MATLAB Function blocks. The reasons:

  1. The mission flies from sea level to ~27 km and from M0 to >M2 over hundreds
     of kilometres of ground track. A flat-earth tangent-plane model accumulates
     large Earth-curvature error in altitude and position at that range.
  2. The vehicle is nearly ballistic in the coast phase, where Earth rotation
     (Coriolis + centrifugal) and the J2 gravity oblateness term are no longer
     negligible.
  3. A QUATERNION attitude state avoids gimbal lock during the steep zoom pull-up
     and any high-pitch manoeuvre, where a Euler-angle integration would be
     singular at theta = +/-90 deg.

So the EqM block integrates in the Earth-Centred Earth-Fixed (ECEF) frame, on
the WGS-84 ellipsoid, with a point-mass + J2 gravity field, and carries attitude
as a unit quaternion.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1.  FRAMES AND NOTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 ECEF (e)  Earth-Centred Earth-Fixed. Origin at Earth centre, rotates WITH the
           Earth at we = 7.292115e-5 rad/s about the z-axis (polar axis).
           This is the integration frame. It is NON-inertial (it rotates), which
           is exactly why Coriolis and centrifugal terms appear (section 3).
 ECI (i)   Earth-Centred Inertial. Non-rotating. True Newtonian frame; we never
           integrate in it, but angular rates are defined relative to it.
 NED (n)   Local North-East-Down tangent frame at the current geodetic point.
           Used for human-readable outputs (Euler angles, flight path, track).
 Body (b)  x forward, y right, z down, fixed to the airframe.

 Attitude is the body->ECEF unit quaternion  q_b2e = [w x y z]  (SCALAR FIRST).
 The equivalent direction-cosine matrix C_b2e rotates a body vector into ECEF:
      v_ecef = C_b2e * v_body.
 Body rates p,q,r are the components of w_b, the body angular rate RELATIVE TO
 THE INERTIAL FRAME, expressed in body axes.

 WGS-84 constants used (identical in every EOM file, so dynamics and outputs are
 consistent):
      we  = 7.292115e-5   rad/s      Earth rotation rate
      mu  = 3.986004418e14 m^3/s^2   GM (gravitational parameter)
      J2  = 1.082626683e-3           2nd zonal harmonic (oblateness)
      Rea = 6378137.0     m          equatorial radius
      f   = 1/298.257223563          flattening,   e2 = f*(2-f)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2.  THE 13-STATE VECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The EqM block integrates a 13-element state (setup_ecef.m / build_ecef_x0.m):

   index   symbol   meaning                                             units
   -----   ------   -------------------------------------------------   ------
   1:3     dr       ECEF position OFFSET from the datum origin r0       m
   4:6     v_e      velocity w.r.t Earth, expressed in ECEF             m/s
   7:10    q_b2e    unit quaternion body->ECEF, scalar-first [w x y z]  -
   11:13   w_b      body angular rate w.r.t inertial, in body [p;q;r]   rad/s

 WHY dr (an offset) instead of the full ECEF position r_e:
 r_e is ~6.37e6 m in magnitude, while the interesting motion is metres to
 hundreds of km. Integrating the small OFFSET dr about a fixed datum origin r0
 (the runway threshold in ECEF) preserves numerical precision — the integrator
 never has to resolve a ~1 m change inside a ~6.4e6 m number. The true position
 is reconstructed only when needed as r_e = r0 + dr.

 The datum origin r0 is the runway threshold converted to ECEF via the standard
 geodetic->ECEF formula (setup_ecef.m):
      N0 = Rea / sqrt(1 - e2*sin(lat0)^2)          (prime vertical radius)
      r0 = [ (N0+h0)*cos(lat0)*cos(lon0);
             (N0+h0)*cos(lat0)*sin(lon0);
             (N0*(1-e2)+h0)*sin(lat0) ]


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.  TRANSLATIONAL DYNAMICS  (ecef_eom_fcn.m, lines 45-49)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Newton's second law written in the ROTATING ECEF frame:

   v_e_dot = C_b2e*(Fb/mass) + g_e  - 2*(we x v_e) - we x (we x r_e)
             \_____________/   \__/    \_________/    \_____________/
              applied specific  grav.    Coriolis       centrifugal
              force (body->ECEF)         acceleration   acceleration

   dr_dot  = v_e

 Term by term:

 (a) C_b2e*(Fb/mass) — the non-gravity body force Fb (aerodynamics + thrust +
     ground reaction) divided by mass to get specific force, rotated from body
     axes into ECEF. Fb is supplied by the ExtForcesMoments subsystem (section 7).

 (b) g_e — gravitational acceleration, POINT-MASS + J2 (lines 42-43):
        g_e = -mu/r^3 * [x;y;z]
              - 1.5*J2*mu*Rea^2/r^5 * [ x*(1-5z^2/r^2);
                                        y*(1-5z^2/r^2);
                                        z*(3-5z^2/r^2) ]
     The first term is spherical Newtonian gravity. The second is the J2
     oblateness correction — the Earth is an ellipsoid, not a sphere, so gravity
     is slightly stronger at the poles and has a small equator-ward component.
     At this mission's altitudes J2 is a small but real effect; including it is
     what makes the coast arc faithful.

 (c) -2*(we x v_e) — the CORIOLIS acceleration. It appears because ECEF rotates:
     a body moving in a rotating frame experiences an apparent sideways
     acceleration. we_e = [0;0;we].

 (d) -we x (we x r_e) — the CENTRIFUGAL acceleration, the outward apparent force
     of the rotating frame. Together (b)+(d) are what a scale actually measures
     as "weight" (gravity minus centrifugal); here they are kept SEPARATE because
     the controller/logging wants the pure gravitational part (section 6).

 THEORY: these Coriolis/centrifugal terms are the price of integrating in a
 non-inertial frame. The alternative — integrating in ECI and transforming — is
 cleaner in physics but forces a time-varying ECI<->ECEF rotation through every
 output; the rotating-ECEF form keeps the Earth-fixed geometry (runway, datum,
 lat/lon) constant and folds the rotation into two closed-form acceleration
 terms instead.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4.  ROTATIONAL DYNAMICS  (ecef_eom_fcn.m, lines 51-53)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Euler's rigid-body equation for angular motion:

      I * w_b_dot = Mb - w_b x (I * w_b)

 solved for the angular acceleration:

      w_b_dot = I^-1 * ( Mb - w_b x (I*w_b) )

 where
   I   = 3x3 inertia tensor [Ixx 0 -Ixz; 0 Iyy 0; -Ixz 0 Izz] (products Ixy,Iyz=0
         by the aircraft's x-z symmetry plane; Ixz retained),
   Mb  = external moment in body axes (aero + thrust offset), from
         ExtForcesMoments,
   w_b x (I*w_b) = the GYROSCOPIC / inertial-coupling term. This is what couples
         roll-pitch-yaw: spinning one axis of a body with unequal moments of
         inertia produces moments on the others (e.g. inertial roll-yaw coupling
         in a fast roll). In code: Iw = I*w_b;  w_b_dot = I \ (Mb - cross(w_b,Iw)).

 I is NOT constant — mass and inertia change as fuel burns and the stage
 separates (MassModel, section 8); the current I is passed into the EqM block
 each step, so Euler's equation is always solved with the right inertia.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5.  ATTITUDE KINEMATICS — QUATERNION PROPAGATION  (lines 55-63)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Attitude is propagated as a quaternion to avoid gimbal lock. The subtlety in a
 rotating-Earth model: the EOM must propagate the body orientation relative to
 the ROTATING ECEF frame, but the body rate w_b is defined relative to INERTIAL.
 So the Earth rate must be removed first:

   w_ie_b = C_b2e' * we_e        (Earth rate expressed in body axes)
   w_eb_b = w_b - w_ie_b         (body rate RELATIVE TO ECEF, in body)

 The quaternion derivative uses the body-relative-to-ECEF rate w_eb_b=[a;bb;c]:

   q_dot = 0.5 * Omega(w_eb_b) * q

 written out (lines 59-62) as the standard quaternion kinematic product. This is
 the rotating-frame analogue of q_dot = 0.5 * q (x) omega.

 UNIT-NORM FEEDBACK (line 63):
   q_dot = q_dot + 1.0*(1 - |q|^2) * q_n
 Numerical integration slowly drifts |q| away from 1. This Baumgarte-style
 feedback term gently pulls the quaternion norm back toward unity every step
 (gain 1.0), keeping C_b2e a proper rotation without a hard renormalisation that
 would inject discontinuities into the derivative. The quaternion is also
 explicitly normalised before use when forming C_b2e (line 32).

 THEORY — why quaternion, not Euler:
 Euler-angle kinematics contain 1/cos(theta) terms that blow up at theta=+/-90
 deg (gimbal lock). The zoom pull-up reaches steep pitch; a quaternion has no
 such singularity and only 4 states with one constraint (|q|=1) handled by the
 norm feedback.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6.  GRAVITY FOR LOGGED WEIGHT & LOAD FACTOR  (ecef_weight_body.m)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The EqM block also outputs the external forces and the load factors for logging.
 It is essential that the gravity used there is the SAME gravity the dynamics
 integrate — otherwise the reported load factor is inconsistent with the motion.

 ecef_weight_body.m computes the gravitational body force using the identical
 point-mass + J2 model:
      g_e     = (point-mass + J2, exactly as in the EOM)
      Fw_body = mass * (C_b2e' * g_e)       gravity force in body axes [N]
      W_mag   = mass * |g_e|                effective-weight scalar   [N]

 W_mag is the load-factor reference, so load factor n = specific-force/g is taken
 against the SAME gravity the dynamics apply. Earth-rotation (centrifugal/
 Coriolis) terms are intentionally NOT folded in here — Fw_body is purely the
 gravitational term that matches g_e in the EOM.

 (Session fix: the old block used flat-earth constant g = 9.80665 pointing down
 in NED — off by ~0.026 m/s^2 plus a direction error. The GravityBody block now
 matches the dynamics gravity to ~1e-10 N. The flight dynamics themselves were
 never flat-earth; only the LOGGED weight was, and that is what was corrected.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 7.  FORCES & MOMENTS INTO THE EqM  (ExtForcesMoments subsystem)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The EqM block consumes two body-axis vectors each step:
   Fb  = aerodynamic force + propulsive thrust + landing-gear reaction   [N]
   Mb  = aerodynamic moment + thrust moment (offset) + gear moment       [N*m]
 Gravity is NOT included in Fb — it enters the dynamics analytically as g_e
 (section 3b), so ExtForcesMoments passes only the NON-gravity forces.

 Sources feeding Fb, Mb:
   * Aerodynamics (Aerovegamidfid): CL/CD/CY and Cl/Cm/Cn from the aero lookup
     tables, scaled by qbar*S (and *b or *c for moments), as functions of
     (alpha, beta, Mach, rates, control deflections).
   * Propulsion (PropulsionFCN): thrust along the body x-axis (with spool lag),
     plus any thrust-offset moment.
   * Landing gear (setup_landing_gear): normal + friction reactions while WoW.

 This clean split — "everything that is not gravity goes in Fb, gravity is
 analytic" — is what keeps the logged weight consistent (section 6) and lets the
 EOM remain a pure Newton-Euler integrator.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 8.  MASS / INERTIA INTO THE EqM  (MassModel)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 mass and Inertia are inputs to the EqM block, updated live:
   * mass integrates -fuel_flow from MASS_full = 28500 kg,
   * Inertia is a 3-point lookup vs mass (full -> separated -> empty),
   * stage separation drops 3000 kg over a 0.5 s ramp (smooth, no inertia step).
 Because both the translational (/mass) and rotational (I^-1) equations use the
 current values, the EOM stays physically correct as the vehicle gets lighter and
 its inertia shrinks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 9.  OUTPUT DERIVATION  (ecef_out_fcn.m)  —  from raw ECEF state to usable signals
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The integrator carries the 13-state in raw ECEF form; ecef_out_fcn.m derives
 everything the rest of the model and the displays need. This is pure algebra
 (no integration):

 9.1  Raw ECEF interface:   r_ecef = r0 + dr,  v_ecef = v_e,  q_b2e, C_b2e, w_b.

 9.2  Geodetic latitude / longitude / altitude (Bowring's method, lines 36-44):
      lon = atan2(y,x); then an iteration-free closed form for geodetic latitude
      on the ellipsoid, and altitude alt = p/cos(lat) - N(lat). Converting the
      ECEF position back to lat/lon/alt is needed for the globe/map, the
      atmosphere model, and altitude-based phase logic.

 9.3  NED frames: builds the ECEF->NED rotation at the CURRENT point (C_e2ned)
      and at the datum (C_e2ned0).

 9.4  Position in NED (lines 52-59): HORIZONTAL components are the datum tangent-
      plane projection, but the VERTICAL component is the TRUE GEODETIC altitude:
         posNED = [pNE(1); pNE(2); -alt]
      Why: a pure tangent-plane altitude drops by the Earth-curvature term
      d^2/(2R) hundreds of km from the runway and would corrupt altitude hold and
      the mission phase logic. Using geodetic alt for the vertical keeps h = -posNED(3)
      correct everywhere.

 9.5  Body velocity relative to Earth:   VB_mps = C_b2e' * v_e.

 9.6  Euler angles relative to local NED (lines 64-70): from C_b2ned = C_e2ned*C_b2e,
         theta = asin(-C_b2ned(3,1))   (pitch, clamped to +/-1 for safety)
         phi   = atan2(C_b2ned(3,2), C_b2ned(3,3))   (roll)
         psi   = atan2(C_b2ned(2,1), C_b2ned(1,1))   (yaw/heading)
      These are OUTPUTS ONLY (for display and some guidance); the state is the
      quaternion, so there is no gimbal-lock risk in the integration.

 9.7  Trajectory signals from NED velocity vned = C_e2ned*v_e (lines 72-77):
         GroundSpeed = sqrt(vN^2 + vE^2)
         Gamma_deg   = atan2(-vD, GroundSpeed)      flight-path angle
         Track_deg   = atan2(vE, vN)                ground track
         Vz          = -vD                          climb rate (+up)
      Gamma is exactly the flight-path angle the gamma-law and glide guidance track.

 9.8  LBE = C_b2ned (body->NED DCM, the "LBE" of the legacy flat-earth EqM) and
      VI_ned = vned are also exported for downstream consumers.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 10.  INITIAL CONDITIONS  (setup_ecef.m / build_ecef_x0.m)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The 13-state IC must be self-consistent with the rotating Earth, or the model
 would "jump" at t=0.

 Ground start (setup_ecef.m):
   * Attitude: phi=0, theta=2 deg, psi=runway heading -> C_b2e0 -> q0.
   * dr = 0 (start AT the datum origin).
   * v_e = 0 (at rest w.r.t Earth).
   * w_b = C_b2e0' * [0;0;we]  — RIGID WITH EARTH. Critical subtlety: "at rest on
     the runway" does NOT mean zero body rate, because w_b is inertial. A vehicle
     bolted to the rotating Earth has the Earth's angular rate; seeding w_b with
     the Earth rate in body axes stops a spurious transient at t=0.

 Airborne trim start (build_ecef_x0.m): same construction but at altitude h_init
 with body-forward speed u_body and pitch theta0, used for the trim/stability
 check (RUN_MODE='trim'). It builds C_b2e from the Euler set, converts to a
 scalar-first quaternion (i_dcm2quat), places dr as the ECEF offset between
 h_init and the datum, sets v_e = C_b2e*[u;0;0], and again seeds w_b with the
 Earth rate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 11.  SOLVER AND NUMERICS  (setup_fcs_sim.m)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The EqM ODE is integrated with:
      SolverName = ode15s  (variable-step, implicit, stiff)
      RelTol = 1e-3, AbsTol = 1e-4, MaxStep = 0.05 s
 ode15s is chosen because the full model is STIFF: fast landing-gear/ground-
 contact dynamics and the 100 Hz controller sit alongside slow trajectory
 dynamics. (This is also why the turbulence model uses DISCRETE Dryden noise —
 continuous noise would force ode15s to tiny steps.) The known rollout solver
 failure at t~1854 s is a stiffness/convergence issue in the gear contact, not an
 error in the EOM itself.

 Numerical safeguards inside the EOM:
   * quaternion normalised before forming C_b2e (avoids a slowly-growing norm
     corrupting the rotation),
   * unit-norm feedback term keeps |q| ~ 1 across the integration,
   * dr-about-datum formulation preserves position precision at Earth-radius
     magnitudes,
   * pitch asin argument clamped to [-1,1] in the output (guards against a
     rounding overshoot at steep attitude).
